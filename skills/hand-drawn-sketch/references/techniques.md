# Hand-Drawn Sketch — Signature Techniques

## 01 · Construction Pass

Light geometry appears first to establish proportion.

**Implementation:** Draw guides with thinner/lighter strokes before final contours.

## 02 · Confident Ink

A darker final line traces or replaces the construction path.

**Implementation:** Use a faster, cleaner draw pass than the exploratory guides.

## 03 · Line Boil

Two or three near-identical path variants alternate frame to frame.

**Implementation:** Author variants or perturb sampled points within a narrow range.

## 04 · Onion-Skin Bounce

Previous and next positions remain faintly visible around a moving object.

**Implementation:** Show 1–2 ghost frames with low opacity.

## 05 · Margin Note

Handwritten note or arrow appears beside the main drawing.

**Implementation:** Keep notes concise and connect them to specific features.

## 06 · Scribble Fill

Rapid hatching fills a region or emphasizes a term.

**Implementation:** Clip repeated hand-like strokes inside the area.

## 07 · Eraser Reveal

Part of a drawing is removed, exposing construction or a corrected idea.

**Implementation:** Use textured mask edges and short debris strokes if needed.

## 08 · Stroke-to-Shape

A hand-drawn curve becomes a clean geometric element in the next scene.

**Implementation:** Match endpoints and silhouette before cleaning the line.

## 09 · Circle in Passes

Circle is drawn 2–3 times with slight offset, then one line is chosen.

**Implementation:** Good for focus or object creation.

## 10 · Flipbook Motion

Movement is shown through a small number of discrete hand-drawn poses.

**Implementation:** Do not tween line art continuously.

## 11 · Underline Sweep

A marker or pencil underline drives emphasis and scene timing.

**Implementation:** Use variable width/opacity to imitate pressure.

## 12 · Note Cluster

Several small labels accumulate around one hero object, then simplify to one conclusion.

**Implementation:** Sequence notes logically rather than appearing simultaneously.

## Selection rule

Use 2–4 techniques for most short scenes. A recognizable system is stronger than a catalogue of effects.
