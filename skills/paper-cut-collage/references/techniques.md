# Paper Cut / Collage — Signature Techniques

## 01 · Paper Drop

A cut element enters with a small positional overshoot and settles like it was placed by hand.

**Implementation:** Use stepped timing or low-frame-rate sampling rather than smooth spring motion.

## 02 · Tape Pin

A strip of tape lands last and appears to secure an element.

**Implementation:** Animate tape from one end or pop it in one frame with a subtle shadow change.

## 03 · Ransom Build

Individual letter scraps assemble into a phrase.

**Implementation:** Vary type source, scale and rotation while maintaining readable word shape.

## 04 · Torn Reveal

A torn paper edge slides away to uncover the next layer.

**Implementation:** Use irregular mask geometry; keep tear direction consistent.

## 05 · Shadow Lift

A paper layer lifts slightly, increasing shadow and scale, then moves.

**Implementation:** Coordinate z-order, shadow blur and translation.

## 06 · Stop-Motion Jitter

An object subtly changes angle/position between held frames.

**Implementation:** Use 2–4 authored variants rather than random noise each frame.

## 07 · Cutout Window

A hole in one paper layer reveals imagery or type below.

**Implementation:** Treat the cutout edge as a physical mask.

## 08 · Sticker Peel

A label corner lifts and peels before transition.

**Implementation:** Animate shape deformation sparingly; preserve paper stiffness.

## 09 · Magazine Sweep

Large printed crops slide like sheets being rearranged on a desk.

**Implementation:** Use overlapping wipes and realistic layer order.

## 10 · Stamp Impact

A graphic or word appears with a compressed, imperfect ink texture.

**Implementation:** Add slight rotation and uneven opacity at the moment of contact.

## 11 · Scissor Path

A dotted or traced path implies a cut before the shape separates.

**Implementation:** Animate path first, then split the piece.

## 12 · Paper-to-Pixel

Paper texture breaks into coarse square blocks that become pixel art.

**Implementation:** Quantize the breakup so the handoff feels intentional.

## Selection rule

Use 2–4 techniques for most short scenes. A recognizable system is stronger than a catalogue of effects.
