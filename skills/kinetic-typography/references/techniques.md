# Kinetic Typography — Signature Techniques

Use these as a vocabulary, not a checklist. Select the smallest combination that produces a distinct scene.

## 01 · Word Slam

A word enters at overscale, compresses on impact, then settles into a readable lockup.

**Implementation:** Animate scaleY and tracking together; add a 1–2 frame overshoot rather than a long elastic bounce.

## 02 · Tracking Explosion

A tight word expands across the frame until letters become compositional anchors.

**Implementation:** Interpolate letter-spacing and x offsets; keep at least one readable midpoint.

## 03 · Split Reveal

Split a word on a semantic or geometric axis and send halves in opposing directions.

**Implementation:** Mask halves independently; reunite on the next beat or reuse the split as a transition.

## 04 · Type Tunnel

Repeat the same word in depth or scale to create a zoom-through corridor.

**Implementation:** Use nested transforms or camera-like scaling; fade only for depth falloff, not as the primary movement.

## 05 · Baseline March

Multiple lines move on alternating baselines at different speeds.

**Implementation:** Create 2–4 lanes; use consistent phase offsets so the result feels choreographed rather than noisy.

## 06 · Counter Morph

Use the counter of O, A, P, R or D as a circle/window/portal.

**Implementation:** Match geometry before morphing; the counter should become the next scene's dominant shape.

## 07 · Outline-to-Fill

Switch between stroked and filled type to create density changes without changing words.

**Implementation:** Time fill changes to impacts; avoid simultaneous stroke-width and scale chaos.

## 08 · Compression Stack

Words squeeze vertically into a stack, then expand into a new hierarchy.

**Implementation:** Animate line-height, scaleY and y-position as one system.

## 09 · Letter Cascade

Letters arrive in sequence but resolve quickly into a word.

**Implementation:** Use stagger based on reading direction; keep total cascade under roughly one-third of the shot.

## 10 · Crop Chase

The frame edge or a mask chases oversized type across the composition.

**Implementation:** Treat the crop as an active editing surface; reveal new words through the same mask.

## 11 · Typographic Pendulum

A line pivots around a punctuation mark or a central glyph.

**Implementation:** Choose a meaningful anchor; rotation should reinforce the phrase structure.

## 12 · Type-as-Shape

Reduce a word into bars, blocks or curves that preserve its proportions, then transform into graphics.

**Implementation:** Sample bounding boxes or strokes so the transition remains visibly derived from type.

## Selection rule

For a short scene, 2–4 techniques are usually enough. If more than five are active simultaneously, simplify unless the deliberate goal is controlled overload.
